# Phase A results: the organic virality model (12 Nov 2026)

Every number here comes from `results/a1_twitter.json`, `results/a2_github*.json` and
`results/a3_twitter.json`. They were generated by the scripts in `phaseA/`, not typed by hand.

## A1. Twitter: what reproduces real Higgs cascades?

**Reconstruction.**
- **Sample:** 20,000 posting accounts, drawn at random.
- **What counts as a cascade:** the distinct accounts that retweeted the poster.
- **How trees are built:** Higgs has no attribution chains, so each adopter's parent is
  inferred from the follow graph as its most recent followee who adopted earlier.
- **External adopters:** 18.6% of adopters follow no earlier adopter (they arrived through
  search, trends and similar routes). They and their descendants are left out of the tree.
- **Same lens for both:** the identical inference is applied to simulated cascades.

**Protocol.** The roots were split in half. A damping factor δ (described below) was chosen
on the calibration half using one statistic: the share of cascades that reach depth 2 or
more. All comparisons below are on the held-out half.

| Held-out half (10,000 roots) | Real | Uniform IC | WC | WC per-account | **WC damped (δ 0.4)** | WC per-account damped (δ 0.6) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Posts with no retweets | 88.9% | 81.8% | 82.8% | 85.9% | 82.8% | 86.2% |
| P(size ≥ 100) | 0.06% | 6.6% | 0.21% | 0.22% | **0.04%** | 0.16% |
| Largest cascade | 785 | 67,469 | 3,241 | 18,780 | **1,200** | 1,050 |
| Cascades reaching depth ≥ 2 | 22.4% | 62.6% | 41.1% | 32.2% | 24.5% (fitted) | 29.4% (fitted) |
| Broadcast share (adopters at depth 1) | 71.1% | 0.0% | 32.0% | 8.9% | **71.4%** | 55.5% |
| Structural virality, median | 2.13 | 99.0 | 2.37 | 2.25 | 2.07 | 2.11 |
| Hours, first to median adoption | 1.00 | 66.1 | 1.29 | 0.86 | 0.68 | 0.66 |
| KS distance: size / structural virality / timing | | 0.36 / 0.74 / 0.71 | 0.14 / 0.20 / 0.11 | 0.04 / 0.13 / 0.12 | **0.04** / 0.15 / 0.22 | **0.03 / 0.05** / 0.18 |

- **Uniform IC is rejected.** Calibrated to the right average rate, it is supercritical: 6.6% of
  posts set off cascades of 100+ adopters, against 0.06% in reality.
- **Calibrated weighted cascades are too viral downstream.** Real Higgs diffusion is broadcast:
  71% of adopters come straight from the poster.
- **Damped adopters fix it.** Letting retweeters pass content on at 0.4× the original poster's
  rate reproduces the held-out broadcast share almost exactly (71.4% vs 71.1%), even though
  that statistic was not used to choose δ. It also gets the cascade-size tail and structural
  virality right. The per-account damped variant fits structural virality best (KS 0.05).
- **Remaining misfits:**
  - Too few silent posts (83–86% against 89%).
  - Cascades unfold somewhat too fast, because delays are resampled from adopter-to-adopter
    edges; the poster's own posting time is unobserved.
- **Organic Twitter model from here on:** weighted cascade on the follower graph, floor 20,
  c = 0.726, adopters damped by δ = 0.4. The per-account damped variant is the sensitivity case.

**Consequence for the virality-BO study.** Its stage 6 used the undamped weighted cascade,
which this check shows is too viral. Under the damped model, top-degree reach falls from
about 24k to about 17k. Rerunning stage 6 under the damped model is a follow-up.

## A2. GitHub: what reproduces organic star arrival?

**Data.** StarScout's matched sample of normal repos: 9,960 repos with ≥ 20 stars, July 2019
to January 2025.

**Contamination.** 954 "normal" repos (9.6%) received 50 to 964 stars within a median of
about 15 minutes. Their stargazers are lockstep-flagged elsewhere 41% of the time, against 24%
for other normal repos. These are likely campaigns StarScout missed, or artifacts. They are
analysed both ways, as "with dumps" and "no dumps".

**Models**, each fitted per repo by maximum likelihood:
- Poisson;
- Hawkes (self-exciting);
- Hawkes plus one exogenous shock at the repo's busiest day (an external post or trending
  listing).

**Checks.**
1. A time-rescaling goodness-of-fit test on each repo.
2. Out of sample: fit on half the repos, generate synthetic repos from the fitted parameter
   population, and compare SignalGraph's burst features with the held-out half.

| | Poisson | Hawkes | Hawkes + shock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Repos passing time-rescaling at 5% (no dumps) | 29.5% | 87.5% | **89.5%** |
| Out-of-sample KS, best 1-day share (no dumps) | 0.46 | 0.21 | **0.02** |
| Out-of-sample KS, best 7 / 30-day share (no dumps) | 0.47 / 0.45 | 0.20 / 0.19 | **0.04 / 0.07** |
| Out-of-sample KS, inter-arrival dispersion (no dumps) | 0.77 | 0.08 | **0.07** |
| Out-of-sample KS, best 1-day share (with dumps) | 0.49 | 0.23 | **0.03** |

- **Organic GitHub model:** Hawkes with an exogenous shock. In the median repo, about half the
  stars are triggered by earlier stars (branching ratio 0.48), and the shock contributes about
  4% of stars, decaying within hours.
- **A detector-relevant finding.** The organic shock model *can* produce repos that get nearly
  all their stars in one day. Trained on the contaminated sample, it reproduces the dumps too.
  So **burst concentration alone cannot separate a campaign from a genuine front-page spike.**
  SignalGraph's current burst score leans on exactly that feature. Phase B tests features that
  can separate the two, such as the aftermath of the burst and its sharpness at minute scale.

## A3. How much of virality is controllable? (Twitter)

**Inside the validated simulator** (law of total variance; 3,000 posting accounts × 30 draws):

| | WC damped | WC per-account damped |
|---|---|---|
| One post: variance in log(1 + reach) explained by *who posts* | 63% | 61% |
| One post: share left to luck | 37% | 39% |
| One post, raw reach: share explained by who posts | 61% | 8% (rare large cascades dominate) |
| 20-seed campaign: luck (sd across draws, % of top-degree reach) | 1.1% | 3.2% |
| 20-seed campaign: spread across seeding strategies (% of top-degree) | ~97% | ~97% |

- **For a single post, luck is large:** about 40% of log-reach variance. With per-account
  heterogeneity it is almost all of the raw-reach variance.
- **For a campaign, luck averages out** (1–3%), and the choice of seeds dominates.
- **"Engineering virality" is real at the level of campaigns and choice of accounts, not of
  individual posts.**

**On the real data, with no simulator** (5-fold cross-validated gradient boosting, R² on
log(1 + direct follow-explained retweets)):

| Prediction task | n | R² | Spearman |
|---|---|---|---|
| All posting accounts: launch information (followers, followees, launch time) | 302,206 | 0.43 | 0.37 |
| All posting accounts: follower count alone | 302,206 | 0.39 | 0.35 |
| Cascades that reached 5 adopters: launch information | 4,278 | 0.49 | 0.58 |
| Same: launch information + first-5-adopter signal | 4,278 | **0.67** | **0.73** |

- **Before launch, almost all the predictable part is audience size.** Launch features add
  about 0.03 R² over follower count.
- **Once a cascade is under way, early signal raises predictability substantially.** This is
  the monitor-then-act advantage (Cheng et al. 2014, unchecked citation).
- **Methods note:** an earlier version counted each account's activity over the whole week as
  a "launch" feature. For accounts never retweeted, that is information from after launch, and
  it inflated R² to 0.60. It was removed.

## What Phase A hands to Phases B and C

- **Organic generators:**
  - Twitter: damped weighted cascade.
  - GitHub: Hawkes plus exogenous shock, fitted on normal repos without the dumps.
- **The key confound:** organic exogenous shocks look like campaigns on burst concentration.
- **Data cautions:**
  - About 10% of StarScout's normal sample is suspicious.
  - Actor profiles were fetched mainly for suspects, so profile features leak the label.
